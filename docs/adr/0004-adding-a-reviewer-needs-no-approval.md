# Adding a reviewer needs no approval; removing one does

After a pact's edit window, removing a reviewer is a change request that a current reviewer must approve, but adding a reviewer applies at once. This looks backwards at first. Any one reviewer can decide an item and the first decision is final, so adding a reviewer is the change that gives the owner more ways to get items approved: they could add a lenient friend, or a second account of their own, who then approves their evidence, excuses, payments and even a request to remove the strict reviewer. We considered making an added reviewer wait for a current reviewer's approval, and rejected it. The honest reason to add a reviewer is that the current one has stopped responding, and review items never expire, so the owner would need approval from the very reviewer who has gone quiet, and every waiting item would stay stuck. The owner also picks every reviewer when creating the pact, so the app never protects against an owner with no honest reviewer, and approval here would protect less than it seems to. Instead, the pact's existing reviewers get a push naming whoever was added (`reviewer-added`, `addReviewersNow` in `src/core/change-requests.ts`), so a reviewer who agreed to hold the owner to the pact can't be routed around without knowing, and can object. Revisit this if penalties are ever paid to someone other than a recipient the owner chose, since then a colluding reviewer would cost someone else. Design review ticket 43 (`.scratch/design-review/issues/43-added-reviewer-can-decide-everything.md`) holds the full analysis.
